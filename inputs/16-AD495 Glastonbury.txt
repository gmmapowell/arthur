&date AD495

The draughts blew throught the Senate hall.

The time had come, Arthur knew.  He had been consulting with his
closest aides for over a week now and they had figured out the entire
plan - in detail - for the next year.  Obviously, he wasn't about to
share the entirety of that plan with the entire Senate, but he had to
tell them enough that they would give him what he needed.

He had two things he knew he could count on.  First, he knew that
Merula - still Leader of the House and by some miracle, still alive -
was completely in his corner, knew and understood the plan - to be
honest, had largely _devised_ the plan over years of scheming - and
was ready to intervene in exactly the right way on his behalf.  Arthur
couldn't shake the feeling that the "right way" might involve him
being personally humiliated, but as long as he achieved what he
wanted, they could pillory him in the town square like a petty thief
for all he cared.

And he knew he could count on their greed and selfishness.  Given the
choice between a quiet, peaceful life and going off to war, they would
choose peace.  Given the choice between fighting and being overrun by
angry Saxons, they would choose to fight.

The preliminaries had been observed and dispensed with.  In truth,
there had been a buzz going around the town for a few days that Arthur
had some great announcement to make and they were not going to like
it.  The senators were waiting with bated breath to hear what it would
be and did not want to wait fussing over details of tax assessments
before they heard what Arthur had to say.  The normal business of this
meeting was tabled for the next session.

Merula, Leader of the House, called on Arthur to speak.

"Gaius Artorius, Dux Bellorum, you are hereby called upon to report
upon your brief, the military and security situation of this island.
It seems," he added with a wry grin, "that much of the island is still
in the hands of the Saxons.  What do you plan to do about that?"

Arthur rose and walked to the middle of the mosaic in the floor of the
chamber which had once been a villa before the Great Raid.  Its owner
had departed for Armorica, and the structure - including the roof -
had been burned and partially destroyed by the Saxons.  Ever
practical, the British had restored it for use as their meeting hall.
Ever miserly, they had done a minimal amount of restoration.  There
was _a_ roof, but it was nothing special.  There were no doors, which
led to a significant swirling wind: through the doors and up through
the holes in the roof.  The hypocaust had not been affected, and
slaves were burning trees by the forest-ful, keeping the floor and
walls warm, but sucking in yet more cold air from outside, leading to
stronger draughts.

Internally, Arthur smiled at the thought that it was a great metaphor
for the state of the island.  Externally he frowned in concentration.
He knew what needed to happen; knew what he needed to say to make it
happen; but needed to find the right tone to convey both the
seriousness of the situation and the optimism he felt about the
opportunity they had _right now_.

He thought about the exact phrasing of Merula's question and nearly
laughed aloud.  Without telling him in advance, Merula had devised the
exact phrasing that was needed to lead Arthur through the field of
caltrops.  How did he do that?  Arthur wondered again.  Was it just a
high level of education and experience?  A mad genius?  Or magic?
Merula always denied having magical abilities, and Arthur wasn't
really superstitious anyway, but sometimes ...

He turned around and focused on the task at hand.

"At this moment, there is nothing I can do about it and, indeed, it
seems to me that the situation is very grave.

"It has come to my attention that the Saxons have decided on another
major raid next Spring, and wish to wipe out our bases and lines of
supply.  They would then turn and try and mop up outstanding
resistance one town at a time."

Around the hall gasps of surprise could be heard from the Senators.
This was news to them.  Unsurprising, since at the current moment it
wasn't, in fact, true.

"How do you know?" "How many?" "When?" "Where?"

Merula rammed his staff on the floor, demanding order.

"Gaius Artorius," he declaimed formally.  "Please continue with your
report."

Arthur walked a few steps across the mosaic, spun and continued.

"Over the past decade, we have felt a stiffening of the resistance
along the border.  We hear that ever more of the Saxons and their
folk, the Angles and the Jutes have come across to join those already
here.

"They have the men and they are starting to lack for land.  Without
our forward resistance on the border, they would have spilt out over
the countryside long ago.

"Everything points to them staging for a raid next year to reduce us.
Logic indicates that it will come this way.  Ever since the Great
Raid, they have known the layout of the country and where our
strengths and weaknesses lie.  And they both lie here, my friends.
They both lie here in the middle west of the island.  You know why."

He paused for a moment, letting them reflect on the reasons.  Then
summarized to drive home the point.

"Our wealth is here.  The engine of our economy is here.  And such
forces as we have are overwhelming based here and supplied from here.

"You ask where would the raid come, and I say, here.

"You ask how, and I say I would expect an attack on three fronts.  I
would expect the Saxons to come from Kent up the main road to the
Thames valley.  They would come along the Thames to <Reading> and then
down through the pass at Swindon to the Afon valley and through Aquae
Sulis and then down to here.  I would expect them to raid, burn and
loot as they went.  Their first mission would be to destroy what we
have left and break my supply lines.  Their secondary mission would be
to take and hold forts, bases and territory as they could."

Arthur walked around again and looked at individual senators: those
that he knew supported him, and those that he knew opposed him.

"What will be destroyed and what will be passed by, I cannot tell
you.  They will come like locusts and they will go where the
resistance is least and the booty is most.  I would imagine that they
would avoid our bases on a first raid, preferring to break up our
supplies.  And with the men we have, it will be hard for us to do much
more than harrass an invasion in strength.

"We hold our own on the border because they have not organized and
mobilized.  If they were to gather together all - or even a large
portion - of their forces we would be unable to oppose them."

And in a rush, he wound up to his peroration.

"In short, my friends, we must conscript.  This coming year will be
decisive.  We cannot allow it to be decided against us."

Once more he turned, and with his long strides returned to his seat to
Merula's left, second among the "consulars", not that he had ever
actually held that rank, but as Dux Bellorum he was considered second
in seniority among the non-officers of the Senate behind its leader.

<Somebody> stood to dispute him.

"You tell us of wars and rumors of wars, and I say if we conscript we
will bring about the ruin of this country.

"We are already being bled dry to provide for the troops you already
have, and now you say that you must conscript all our young men to go
hold a border for goodness knows how long against superior numbers.
This is madness.

"I tell you, we can’t afford to keep this country on a war footing
forever.  It will bankrupt us."

Merula stood and looked around him.  Then he focused his eyes firmly
on <whoever> and said, softly:

"I quite agree."

The hall was silent for moments, that stretched into a significant
pause.  What did this intervention mean?  Merula was known to be in
the same faction as Arthur; the only question was who was in who's
pocket: who was master and who follower.

In that deep silence, the whispered question sounded like a roar.

"What?"

"We cannot continue this war forever.  We need more time for the study
of philosophy.  Children should have time to grow up being children.
We need to respect the rule of law and we need to curb the abuses and
indignities of these 'regional governors' who seem to think they are
above the law."

He turned to Arthur.

"What are you going to do about it?  It's your job, you know, this
keeping us safe thing."

Arthur had thought he was ready for what Merula was going to do, but
this felt like a stabbing betrayal.  He jumped up and strode forward
as Merula returned to his seat and gently lowered himself into it,
suddenly managing to look old and frail as he did so.

Arthur spat out the words, directed at Merula but hitting everyone in
that cavernous hall.  "Do you think I like nearly getting killed every
week?  Making inconsequential raids that just spur more retaliation?
No, what we need to do is to gather an army and fight one battle that
will ensure peace for a generation."

He stopped, paused and turned.  The white heat of rage left him and an
almost battle-like stillness descended.

"No, my friends, this is not a conscription forever.  This is one year
and one year only.  This time next year, I will return here and will
resign my title as Dux Bellorum.  If I am successful, I will then run
for the very proper job of Consul and earn my true right to occupy
my seat among the consulars.

"I am not going to use these new forces to reinforce the border and
have more ineffective raids.  No, my plan is very simple.  I will let
the Saxons gather.  I will let the invade.  I will let them come down
the Thames, but I will only let them come the way I want them to
come.  They will loot and burn, but we will have cleared out
everything along their route.  We will harrass them from our hilltops
and we will force them along the way until they are forced into a
position of disadvantage.

"And then they will have to fight a battle of my choosing.  If the
troops are there, my friends, we will win.

"We will win, my friends.  I guarantee it."

Arthur stood in the centre of the mosaic and slowly turned around.  As
he did so, his eyes lingered here and there, but afterwards each and
every senator felt that he was looking straight at him.

Hours later, a division was held and the motion was carried
unanimously: a general conscription of the British would be held, and
held immediately.  They would form three legions, which would be
handed to Arthur for training.  One would form at Gloucester, one at
Badonicus and one at Isca Dumnorium.

One thought was in every senator's minds as they left that evening:
the Saxons were coming and the only choice was whether they would
destroy everything in their path or whether Arthur would be able to
successfully resist them.

---

The problem for Arthur was that the Saxons had no intention of coming.

Much of what he had said had been true or grounded in truth.  More and
more of the Saxon "volk" had come across the Channel from Germany
until their homelands in northern Europe had been deserted to the
tribes behind them - the Franks, the Huns and a smattering of Gothic
tribes who had become detached from the main bodies who had overrun
Gaul and Hispania and were now said to be in Italy and Africa.

Arthur certainly had enough first-hand experience to know that there
were swelling numbers of armed Saxons all along the border territories
where he fought from Linnius in the north down to the Arun in the
south.  And he knew that sooner or later they would break out, either
in one big raid, or just in an effort to take a small piece of land at
a time.

And he knew that the only way to "contain" them would be to have the
kind of general conscription and armed forces along the border that he
had rejected.  With three legions, it was possible he could even
invade the Saxon lands; but if he were to do that, he would be handing
the territorial advantage to the Saxons.  It would be easier to beat
them if he were to bring them to a prepared battlefield of his
choosing.  And it would be prepared.  He had spent almost twenty years
preparing both the battlefield and for the battle.

The only remaining problem was to get the Saxons to do what he
wanted.  He had made it sound so easy, but in reality, his plan was
devilishly cunning and required everybody to play their part and stick
together.  Treachery at this point could be fatal.
