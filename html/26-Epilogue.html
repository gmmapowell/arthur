Epilogue

Arthur was dead.

And so, the world Arthur had created, and in large part held together through the force of his own personal will, started to fall apart.

The immediate causes picked up directly from the death of Arthur: the Saxons no longer trusted the Romans to be honest partners, but saw them as desirous of being their overlords once again. They might still be barbarians, but they had their pride, and that was more important to them than the common good of the "people of Britain".

The Romano-British might have been able to resist the Saxons - as Ambrosius and Arthur had done - if that had been their united goal. But the stresses already implied in the fall of Arthur - the competition between the rich and mighty, and their own self-interest - fractured completely with Arthur's death and the rebellion of the Saxons.

Some of Arthur's generals did well for themselves and held on to their territory - Dyfnwal and his descendants held the border against all comers - English and highland Scots - for another 500 years. Cunedda held central Wales down to the time of Edward I. And Theoderic and his descendants successfully held off the advance of the English at the border of Cornwall.

But across the rest of the island, the Saxons, Angles and Jutes - combined as the English - managed to push back the boundaries of the British kings and picked them off one by one, until they had control of the entire island - just in time to be attacked by the next influx of seaborne raiders, the Vikings.

But that would be another story.
